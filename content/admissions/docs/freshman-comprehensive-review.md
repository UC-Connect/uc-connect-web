# How UC Reviews First-Year Applications (Comprehensive Review)

UC campuses use comprehensive review with 13 factors for first-year applicants. All campuses use the same factors but may weigh them differently, so students should check each campus's own selection information.

## The 13 first-year review factors

1. GPA in all completed A-G courses, including extra points for completed UC-certified honors courses.
2. Number, content, and performance in A-G courses beyond the minimum requirements.
3. Number and performance in UC-approved honors, AP, IB Higher Level, and transferable college courses.
4. Ranking in the top 9 percent of the high school class at the end of junior year (Eligibility in the Local Context, ELC).
5. Quality of the senior-year program, based on the type and number of A-G courses in progress or planned.
6. Quality of academic performance relative to the opportunities available at the student's high school.
7. Outstanding performance in one or more specific subject areas.
8. Outstanding work in one or more special projects in any academic field.
9. Recent, marked improvement in GPA and quality of coursework.
10. Special talents, achievements, awards, special skills, special interests, and leadership experiences, such as community service or student government.
11. Completion of special projects within the high school curriculum or related school events or programs.
12. Academic accomplishments in light of life experiences and circumstances, such as disabilities, low family income, first-generation status, need to work, difficult family situations, refugee status, or veteran status.
13. Location of the student's secondary school and residence.

## How campuses review

- Each campus reviews applications independently and does not know the student's status at other UC campuses.
- Campuses do not consider where else a student applied.
- UC does not discriminate against or give preferential treatment based on race, sex, color, ethnicity, or national origin.
- The academic performance needed for a campus or major changes from year to year because the applicant pool changes.

## Standardized tests (SAT/ACT)

The UC Board of Regents decided in 2020 that UC will not consider SAT or ACT scores in admissions decisions or in awarding Regents and Chancellor's scholarships. UC's current first-year requirements do not list an SAT or ACT requirement. UC stated that scores may still be used in limited ways, such as an alternative way to meet minimum requirements or for course placement after enrollment.

Source for the test policy: https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/press-room/university-california-board-regents-unanimously-approved-changes-standardized-testing

## Answer guardrails

- Do not rank the 13 factors or claim one factor matters most; campuses weigh them differently.
- Do not predict admission chances from any combination of factors.
- Do not tell students SAT or ACT scores will help their UC admission decision.
