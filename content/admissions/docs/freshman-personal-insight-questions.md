# UC Personal Insight Questions for First-Year Applicants

First-year applicants answer 4 of 8 personal insight questions (PIQs). Each response can be up to 350 words. UC states that all questions are equal: no question is weighted more than another, and choosing particular questions gives no advantage or disadvantage.

## The 8 prompt topics (paraphrased)

1. Leadership: a leadership experience where you positively influenced others, helped resolve disputes, or contributed to group efforts over time. Leadership can be informal.
2. Creativity: how you express your creative side, such as problem solving, original thinking, or the arts.
3. Greatest talent or skill: how you developed and demonstrated it over time.
4. Educational opportunity or barrier: how you took advantage of a significant educational opportunity or worked to overcome an educational barrier.
5. Significant challenge: the challenge, the steps you took to overcome it, and how it affected your academic achievement.
6. Inspiring academic subject: a subject that inspires you and how you have pursued it inside or outside the classroom.
7. Community: what you have done to make your school or community a better place.
8. Strength as a candidate: beyond what you have already shared, what makes you a strong candidate for UC.

## UC's writing tips

- Start early and leave time to revise.
- Use specific examples instead of lists of accomplishments.
- Use "I" and "my" statements.
- Grammar and spelling are not scored, but errors can distract readers.
- Feedback is allowed, but answers must be the applicant's own work with no plagiarized or borrowed text.
- Save answers as plain text before pasting them into the application.

The additional comments section is for unusual circumstances or unclear items in the application and should not be used as another essay.

PIQ responses are one of many factors in review and cannot by themselves determine an admission decision.

## Answer guardrails

- Do not write a PIQ response for the student; UC requires the answers to be the student's own work.
- Do not claim some prompts are better or preferred by admissions readers.
- Do not quote prompt wording beyond the paraphrases above; direct students to the official page for exact wording.
