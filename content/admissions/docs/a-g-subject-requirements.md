# UC A-G Subject Requirements

First-year applicants must complete 15 yearlong college-preparatory courses, called A-G courses, with a grade of C or better. At least 11 of the 15 must be completed before the start of the last year of high school.

Except for mathematics and language other than English, courses must be completed in grades 9 through 12. Math and language courses taken in 7th or 8th grade can count if the high school accepts them.

California students must take courses on their school's UC-approved A-G course list, which can be checked on UC's A-G course list website.

## A - History (2 years required)

- 1 year of world history, cultures, or historical geography;
- 1 year of U.S. history, or one-half year of U.S. history plus one-half year of civics or American government.

## B - English (4 years required)

Four years of college-preparatory English that includes frequent writing and reading of classic and modern literature. No more than one year of ESL-type courses can be used.

## C - Mathematics (3 years required, 4 years recommended)

Covers elementary algebra, advanced algebra, and two- and three-dimensional geometry. Geometry, or an integrated math course with sufficient geometry content, is always required. UC states that one transferable college course will not satisfy the full three-year math requirement.

## D - Science (2 years required, 3 years recommended)

Two years covering two of the three subjects biology, chemistry, and physics. One year of approved interdisciplinary or earth and space science can satisfy one year. Computer science, engineering, and applied science courses can count as an additional science (third year and beyond).

## E - Language Other Than English (2 years required, 3 years recommended)

Two years of the same language, or equivalent to the second level of high school instruction. American Sign Language, classical languages such as Latin and Greek, and Native American languages are accepted.

## F - Visual and Performing Arts (1 year required)

One yearlong course in dance, music, theater, visual arts, or interdisciplinary arts, or two one-semester courses in the same discipline.

## G - College-Preparatory Elective (1 year required)

One year (two semesters) chosen from G-area courses or from A-F courses beyond those used to meet the A-F requirements.

## Meeting A-G with AP or IB exams

Examples stated by UC:
- History: a 3, 4, or 5 on AP U.S. History or AP African American Studies (U.S. history); a 3-5 on AP European History, AP World History: Modern, or AP Human Geography (world history).
- English: a 3-5 on AP English Language and Composition or AP English Literature and Composition; a 5-7 on IB English: Literature HL.
- Mathematics: a 3-5 on AP Calculus AB or BC, or a 4-5 on AP Precalculus, satisfies two years but not geometry.
- Science: a 3-5 on two AP exams from different disciplines among Biology, Chemistry, Physics, and Environmental Science.
- Language other than English: a 3-5 on an AP Language and Culture exam (Chinese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Spanish, or Latin) or AP Spanish Literature and Culture; a 5-7 on an IB HL language B exam.
- Visual and performing arts: a 3-5 on AP Art History, AP Art and Design, or AP Music Theory.
- Elective: a 3-5 on one of AP Computer Science A, AP Computer Science Principles, AP Microeconomics, AP Macroeconomics, AP Psychology, AP U.S. Government, or AP Comparative Government.

## Meeting A-G with college courses

College courses used for A-G must:
- be from a college accredited by one of the seven former regional accrediting agencies;
- be UC-transferable (English and math are exceptions);
- be worth at least 3 semester units or 4 quarter units;
- clearly fall within an A-G subject area;
- be taken for a letter grade with a C (2.0) or better.

## Answer guardrails

- Do not confirm that a specific high school course is A-G approved; tell the student to check their school's list on the UC A-G course list site.
- Do not say AP Calculus satisfies the geometry requirement.
- Do not treat recommended years (4th year of math, 3rd year of science or language) as minimum requirements, but note that they are recommended.
