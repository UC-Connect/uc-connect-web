# UC First-Year GPA Requirement and Calculation

## Minimum GPA

- California residents: 3.0 in A-G courses.
- Nonresidents: 3.4 in A-G courses.

UC calculates the official UC GPA when the application is submitted. A self-calculated GPA is only an estimate.

## How the UC GPA is calculated

1. Use A-G courses completed from the summer after 9th grade through the summer after 11th grade. 9th grade and 12th grade grades are not included.
2. Convert letter grades to points: A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1. Pluses and minuses do not count.
3. Add honors points (California residents): one extra point per semester of a UC-recognized honors course, such as AP, IB Higher Level, designated IB Standard Level, UC-transferable college courses, or UC-certified honors courses.
   - Honors points are capped at 8 total across 10th and 11th grades.
   - No more than 4 honors points may come from 10th grade.
   - A D or F in an honors course earns no extra point.
   - The summer after 9th grade and the summer after 10th grade count as 10th grade; the summer after 11th grade counts as 11th grade.
4. Divide total grade points, including honors points, by the number of letter grades earned in those courses. Do not round up or down.

## Honors weighting for nonresidents

For the 3.4 nonresident minimum, UC gives honors weight only to AP and IB courses, and only for grades of A, B, or C. School-designated honors courses are not weighted for the 3.4 calculation but may be considered in comprehensive review.

## Answer guardrails

- Do not tell a student their exact official UC GPA; a self-calculated GPA is an estimate.
- Do not include 9th grade or 12th grade grades in the UC GPA calculation.
- Do not exceed the honors point caps (8 total, at most 4 from 10th grade).
- Never describe the minimum GPA as a competitive GPA for any campus or major.
