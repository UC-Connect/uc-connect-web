# How UC Reviews Transfer Applications (Comprehensive Review)

When there are more qualified transfer applicants than available spaces, UC campuses select students using comprehensive review. Junior-level transfer students from California community colleges are given priority consideration for admission.

## The 9 transfer review factors

1. Completion of a specified pattern or number of courses that meet breadth or general education requirements.
2. Completion of a specified pattern or number of courses that provide continuity with upper-division courses in the major, such as UC Transfer Pathways, associate degrees for transfer (ADT, California community colleges only), and campus-specific major prerequisites.
3. GPA in all transferable courses, especially courses in a UC Transfer Pathway or major prerequisites.
4. Participation in academically selective honors courses or programs.
5. Special talents, achievements, and awards in a particular field, plus leadership and other achievements that show promise to contribute to campus life.
6. Completion of special projects undertaken in the context of the college curriculum.
7. Academic accomplishments in light of life experiences and special circumstances, such as disabilities, low family income, first-generation status, need to work, difficult family situations, or refugee or veteran status.
8. Location of the college and the applicant's residence.
9. Completion of a UC Transfer Pathway or an associate degree for transfer (ADT) offered by a California community college.

## How campuses review

Each campus reviews an application without knowing its status at other UC campuses and does not consider where else the student applied.

## Answer guardrails

- Do not rank these factors or claim one matters most.
- Do not say an ADT or a completed Transfer Pathway guarantees UC admission; they are review factors.
- Do not predict admission chances.
